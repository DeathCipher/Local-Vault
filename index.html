What LocalVault does

It does the everyday jobs of a password manager and keeps everything on the computer you installed it on.

Encrypts your vault
Your logins are stored in a single file protected with AES-256. The key comes from your master password, and nothing is ever sent over the network.
Locks itself
After 5 minutes without activity the vault locks and asks for your master password again.
Clears the clipboard
A password you copy is removed from the clipboard after 20 seconds, and as soon as the vault locks.
Creates strong passwords
Generate a random password of the length you choose, and watch the strength meter as you type your own.
Backs up and imports
Save an encrypted backup that’s still locked with your master password. You can also export a plain CSV to move to another manager (it isn’t encrypted, so delete it afterwards), and import logins from a CSV.
Works offline
There’s nothing to sign in to and nothing to update online. Install it once and it keeps working.

Install it in four steps

  1. Download the installer

    Use the Download button above. The file is called localvault.msi.

  2. Open it

    Windows may say it doesn’t recognise the publisher, because the installer isn’t code-signed. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

    Why Windows shows this warning

  3. Choose where it goes

    It installs to Program Files by default. Use Browse to pick another folder, and untick Desktop shortcut if you don’t want an icon on your desktop.

  4. Create your master password

    Open LocalVault from the Start menu or desktop and choose a master password. It must be at least 12 characters and hard to guess. There’s no way to recover it if you forget it.
