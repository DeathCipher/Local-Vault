How LocalVault protects you

What it does with your passwords, and the limits you should know about before you rely on it.

What it does

Your logins are stored in a single file on your PC, encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key is made from your master password using scrypt, a method designed to make guessing passwords slow. LocalVault never connects to the internet, and nothing leaves your computer.

It insists on a master password of at least 12 characters that is hard to guess, because the whole vault is only as strong as that one password.

There is no password recovery. There’s no account or server that could reset your master password, so if you forget it, the vault can’t be opened.

What it can’t protect against

  • Memory on a running PCPrograms written in Python can’t wipe secrets from memory. “Locked” means the vault is closed in the app, not erased from RAM, so someone who copies the memory of the running program could find your key or passwords.
  • Swapping in an old vault fileAnyone who can write to your vault file could replace it with an older valid copy, including the backup, without knowing your password. A single file on its own can’t detect that.
  • Clipboard historyLocalVault clears what it copies after 20 seconds and when it locks. Windows clipboard history (Win+V) and cloud clipboards can still keep their own copy, so turn those off for best results.
  • Changing your master passwordThis deletes the backup file, because the backup is still protected by your old password.
